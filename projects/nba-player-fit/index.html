01 — PROJECT OVERVIEW

The project

When a team considers adding a player, raw production is only half the question — the other half is fit: does the player's style match how the team actually plays?

This project explores that question quantitatively, examining how well an NBA player fits a team's style of play across pace, transition offense, shooting profile, play style, and defense, weighed against team needs.

It is the kind of analysis useful to front-office analysts, sports-media analysts, and anyone building player-evaluation tools: a structured way to argue about compatibility instead of relying on reputation or counting stats alone.

Documentation status: this is the least-documented project in the archive. No public repository, dataset export, or screenshots were available to verify when this page was written, so this page describes the project's scope and method at the level the homepage supports — and deliberately reports no invented metrics, datasets, or findings. Specifics will be added when the underlying notebooks/data are published.

02 — APPROACH

Analytical approach

Style dimensions

Compare player and team profiles across pace, transition frequency, shooting distribution, play style, and defensive characteristics — the dimensions along which a signing can clash even when both sides are individually strong.

Fit interpretation

Frame results as compatibility arguments (where a player amplifies a team identity vs. where he duplicates or conflicts with it), rather than a single reductive 'fit score' presented without context.

Tools as listed for the project: Python · SQL · data analysis · sports analytics. No specific libraries, data providers, or seasons are claimed here because they could not be verified from published source material.

03 — CHALLENGES & LIMITATIONS

Challenges & limitations

Fit is multidimensional

Challenge: A player can fit a team’s pace but not its shot diet, or its defense but not its creation burden.

Decision: Analyse several style dimensions separately before synthesising an interpretation, instead of collapsing everything into one number up front.

Unpublished source material

Challenge: Without a public repo or data export, results cannot be independently checked.

Decision: Publish only the supported scope on this page and flag the gap — the honest next step is releasing the notebooks and data definitions.
