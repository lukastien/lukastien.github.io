01 — PROJECT OVERVIEW

The business problem

For an installment / BNPL-style lender, the collections budget is finite: intervene too late and losses are written off; intervene everywhere and you spend more than you save. The operational questions are who is at risk, when risk concentrates, and where a collections dollar earns the most back.

This project models default risk on real consumer-credit data, scores a quarter-million borrowers into risk tiers, and prices an early-intervention strategy by tier.

Framing honesty (from the repo): the data is the real public Give Me Some Credit credit dataset, reframed with a BNPL lens — it is not Affirm/Klarna/Afterpay transaction data. And the dataset has no observed time-to-default: the survival duration is constructed by a documented deterministic rule (see below), so Kaplan–Meier curves illustrate the tiering, not real observed timing.

What drives risk

Prior delinquency count is the #1 XGBoost feature (importance 0.408), followed by revolving-utilisation bucket (0.284). An explicitly engineered payment-to-income feature ranked only #11 — a useful negative result, reported as such.

Tiering

The scored population splits Low 176,052 (70%) / Medium 50,300 (20%) / High 25,151 (10%). At the top decile, precision is 0.372 and recall 0.557 (PR-AUC 0.404) — honest numbers for a 6.68% base default rate.

Collections economics (scenario)

Under documented assumptions ($15/attempt × 2, 20% success, 70% LGD, ~$9,789 average balance proxy), prioritising High then Medium tiers yields a modelled net benefit of ~$29.0M (High) and ~$66.3M (High+Medium). These are scenario outputs, sensitive to score calibration — see the repo strategy brief caveats.

Constructed-time survival

On the constructed duration, Kaplan–Meier S(24) is 0.985 (Low), 0.907 (Medium), 0.620 (High) — the tier ordering behaves as intended. Cox PH was not run in this repo.

Record-count discipline

Feature engineering and scoring run on the combined 251,503 records; classifier training/evaluation use only the 150,000 labelled training rows (stratified 80/20). 'Engineer and score on 200K+; learn and validate only on labelled data.'

Feature engineering

Payment-to-income ratio, prior-delinquency count (with 96/98 artifact-code handling), and revolving-utilisation buckets — each documented with its role and measured importance.

Constructed duration rule

Window 24 months; non-events censored at 24; for events, duration = clip(24 − 2 × severity, 1, 23) where severity combines prior delinquencies and utilisation. Deterministic, documented, and never described as observed time-to-default.

04 — CHALLENGES & DECISIONS

Challenges, decisions & limitations

No observed default timing

Challenge: A 'when will they default?' model needs event times the dataset simply does not contain.

Decision: Construct a transparent, deterministic duration proxy for Kaplan–Meier illustration, label it everywhere, and skip Cox PH rather than fit it to invented times.

Headline-count inflation risk

Challenge: '200K+ records' is true for scoring but false for training.

Decision: Publish the record-count policy in the README and repeat it wherever the number appears.

Scenario dollars can mislead

Challenge: Collections savings scale directly with assumed success rates, LGD, and balance proxies.

Decision: State every assumption next to the dollar figures and flag calibration sensitivity in the strategy brief.
