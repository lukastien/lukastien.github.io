01 — PROJECT OVERVIEW

The business problem

Employee turnover is expensive — replacing a departing employee costs recruiting time, training time, and lost productivity. But most retention effort is spread evenly, because managers cannot see which employees are at risk or why.

This project answers three questions on a real public HR dataset: which employees are most likely to leave, what factors drive that risk, and how much a targeted retention programme could realistically save.

It is useful to HR analysts and people managers as a template for evidence-based retention: segment the workforce, quantify risk, price the intervention, and only then recommend action.

Dataset: the public IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition & Performance dataset — 1,470 employees, 35 features. A self-directed portfolio project; results are illustrative, not advice for a specific employer.

Data preparation & SQL

Designed a relational SQL schema (sql/schema.sql) and documented queries (sql/queries.sql) computing attrition rates by department, tenure, overtime, and salary band — so the headline rates are reproducible SQL, not notebook-only numbers.

Modeling

src/model_training.py trains a logistic-regression baseline and an XGBoost classifier, compares held-out ROC-AUC, inspects feature importance (overtime ranks first in both), and exports per-employee risk scores.

Cost-of-turnover model

An Excel workbook (reports/cost_of_turnover.xlsx, built by src/build_cost_model.py) flags the 31 current employees at ≥45% predicted risk and prices a targeted retention pilot against turnover cost.

Reporting

Tableau-ready CSV extracts (dashboard/) with a build guide, plus a strategy brief (reports/strategy_brief.md) written for non-technical stakeholders.

04 — CHALLENGES & DECISIONS

Challenges & decisions

A simpler model beat the complex one

Challenge: XGBoost (0.75 ROC-AUC) underperformed logistic regression (0.81) on the held-out test set.

Decision: Report both honestly and lead with the baseline. On 1,470 rows, a regularised linear model generalising better than a boosted tree is a plausible, publishable result — not a failure to hide.

Turning probabilities into money

Challenge: A risk score alone does not justify budget; a savings claim without assumptions invites scepticism.

Decision: Document the cost model's assumptions inside the workbook and label ~$455K as projected. The recommendation is a bounded pilot on 31 flagged employees, not a company-wide programme.

Class imbalance

Challenge: Only 16.1% of employees left, so accuracy would reward predicting 'stays' for everyone.

Decision: Evaluate with ROC-AUC and a risk threshold for flagging (≥45%) instead of raw accuracy.
