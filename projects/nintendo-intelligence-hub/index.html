Nintendo publishes decades of hardware, software, regional, and financial results in investor-relations pages and workbooks — authoritative data that is remarkably easy to analyse wrong. Cumulative columns masquerade as quarters, platform families already contain their component models, and regional definitions change over time. Sum the wrong rows and a dashboard reports confident, incorrect numbers.

Nintendo Intelligence Hub is the pipeline, warehouse, and analytics layer that makes those figures safe to use: automated ingestion with provenance, a PostgreSQL warehouse, tested dbt marts, a live dashboard, monitoring, and honestly backtested forecasts.

01 — PROJECT OVERVIEW

The business problem

A BI analyst covering a platform business needs trustworthy answers: How did hardware and software sell-in move by platform and region? How do unit trends relate to consolidated revenue and margin? Which platforms are growing, maturing, or in tail decline?

Nintendo's disclosures answer all of this — spanning consoles from historical systems to the Switch family, with consolidated financials back to FY3/1991. But the source material mixes web pages and historical workbooks, cumulative year-to-date spans, life-to-date snapshots beside annual flows, and regional layouts whose definitions shift across decades.

The platform treats correctness of grain and provenance as the product: every mart row traces to a source document, every total states what it includes, and every derived number carries the rule that produced it.

That is the same discipline a BI, analytics-engineering, or data-engineering team needs in production — connecting data engineering directly to decisions about platform strategy, regional performance, and financial planning. The intended users are analysts and decision-makers who need reported figures they can defend, not just chart.

04 — DATA ENGINEERING

Engineering the warehouse

Sourcing & ingestion

Genuine Nintendo IR material only: the hardware/software unit-sales page, top-selling title pages, and historical regional-sales and consolidated-income workbooks. Loads are per-document and transactional; a failed download changes nothing, and unavailable sources are reported — never substituted.

Cleaning & validation

Per-source parsers handle decades of layout drift with rejection accounting — every rejected cell is explained. Negative disclosed values are preserved as reported (legitimate returns and revisions), not “cleaned” away.

PostgreSQL schema design

Layered schemas separate concerns: ref dimensions, raw_official disclosures as printed, dbt staging views, mart_reported analytics, plus meta (document registry, pipeline runs, monitoring) and a separate forecast schema so predictions never contaminate reported facts.

dbt transformations

Five documented marts: annual platform performance, regional performance, hardware/software trends, lifetime snapshots, and financial performance. Fiscal logic is implemented once in Python and mirrored in SQL — Nintendo's year ends March 31, and quarters are derived only from adjacent year-to-date spans.

Data quality testing

72 dbt results (schema and singular tests reconciling marts back to disclosures) plus 100+ Python tests covering fiscal logic, parsing, idempotency, monitoring lifecycle, dashboard calculations, and forecast leakage.

Reproducibility

Checksum-based loads make reruns no-ops (verified: 2,323 records unchanged). Docker Compose provisions PostgreSQL 16; one command runs the full pipeline, and the forecasting job is independently runnable with a versioned model run.

06 — FORECASTING & PREDICTIVE ANALYTICS

Forecasting, honestly evaluated

Forecasting was included to answer a planning question — what does a defensible baseline extrapolation of platform and financial performance look like? — and, just as importantly, to demonstrate how model claims should be validated before anyone quotes them.

Nine forecasting models evaluated, from naive baselines to ARIMA
FamilyModelsNotes
BaselinesNaive (last value) · Historical mean · 3-year moving average · Drift · Linear trendAlways available; genuinely competitive on short, lifecycle-dominated annual series.
Statistical (statsmodels)Simple exponential smoothing · Holt trend · Damped Holt · ARIMA(1,1,0)Minimum-observation gates (≥5, ≥6, ≥6, ≥12). Fit failures are counted, never silently replaced.

Expanding-window backtesting

Each fold trains only on years strictly before its target year, and is scored only when the target directly follows the training window — 980 scored folds in total. A spy-model test proves training never sees the target year.

Model comparison & selection

Models are compared on MAE, RMSE, bias, and (where meaningful) WAPE/sMAPE. Selection needs ≥3 folds and prefers a simpler model within 10% of the best RMSE — on consolidated net sales, the naive forecast wins, and the system reports that.

Three-year forecasts

Eligible series receive a 3-year horizon stored in the separate forecast schema with the selected model, fold count, and metrics attached. Retired platforms (16 series) and the launch-year Switch 2 series are excluded by rule rather than forecast with invented confidence; only 8 of 26 series are forecast.

Uncertainty & limitations

80% intervals use a normal approximation over backtest RMSE. They are approximate and not empirically calibrated: leakage-free historical coverage is 63% on consolidated net sales against the 80% nominal band, and that gap is reported, not hidden. Annual series are short and lifecycle-dominated, so forecasts are baseline extrapolations — not guaranteed predictions and never Nintendo guidance.

07 — MONITORING & DATA QUALITY

Monitoring that earns trust

Automated pipeline checks

Every pipeline run evaluates pipeline, data-quality, business-movement, and freshness rules after the dbt build. A monitoring failure fails the run rather than passing silently.

Validation & reconciliation

Checks cover duplicate grains, schema drift, missing documents, and regional reconciliation against the disclosed worldwide total. A live evaluation flags exactly the four regional reconciliations exceeding documented thresholds.

Freshness & anomaly detection

Historical disclosures are judged on their publication schedule, not daily age. Annual business movements are scored with YoY and MAD-based robust statistics; known legitimate conditions (negative revisions, incomplete 1990s breakdowns) are excluded by construction.

Alert deduplication & lifecycle

Alerts are fingerprint-deduplicated (re-running creates 0 duplicates) with an audited acknowledge / resolve / reopen lifecycle. The system even resolves its own stale failure alert once the pipeline is healthy again.

Testing backs this up: 100+ Python tests and 72 dbt results, 0 failures — including tests that fail the build if synthetic data ever appears in an official mart.

08 — TECHNICAL CHALLENGES

Challenges & engineering decisions

1 · Cumulative spans masquerading as quarters

Challenge: Nintendo's Q1–Q3 columns are cumulative year-to-date spans, not quarters — and there are no quarterly financial columns to fabricate from.

Decision: A span-aware period model implemented in Python and mirrored as a dbt macro; incremental quarters are derived only from adjacent YTD spans. The quarterly marts are honestly empty, and the dashboard says so instead of inventing quarterly values.

2 · Preventing forecasting data leakage

Challenge: With 10–36 annual observations per series, a single leaked target year would flatter every metric.

Decision: Expanding-window folds train strictly before the target, score only gap-free one-year-ahead folds, and are guarded by a spy-model test. Missing years stay missing — never zero-filled — and life-to-date snapshots never enter a training set.

3 · Heterogeneous reporting formats across decades

Challenge: Workbook layouts, printed scales, and regional definitions drift over 35+ years; family totals already include component models, and GLOBAL already includes regions.

Decision: Per-source parsers with rejection accounting, scale-aware reconciliation thresholds, and structural safeguards (include_in_cross_platform_total, never combining regional layouts) enforced by dbt tests — so double-counting is impossible by construction, not by analyst care.

4 · Separating official observations from generated forecasts

Challenge: Forecasts or synthetic demo data leaking into reported figures would destroy the project's credibility.

Decision: Forecasts live only in the forecast schema, synthetic rows only in raw_simulated, and dbt tests fail if a synthetic label appears in an official mart. The dashboard reads stored forecast runs and never retrains.

5 · Safe read-only access for a public demo

Challenge: A deployed dashboard must never be able to write facts, read raw schemas, or retrain models.

Decision: A verified PostgreSQL read-only role (nih_dashboard) with SELECT only on marts/forecast/meta, a connection limit and statement timeout, and default privileges that survive dbt view rebuilds. Deployment is fully prepared; going live awaits the owner's cloud accounts.

09 — RESULTS & TAKEAWAYS

What this demonstrates

Delivered: an end-to-end platform that ingests 2,323 official records idempotently, transforms them into five tested dbt marts, serves a six-page live dashboard and Tableau-ready exports, monitors its own data quality with deduplicated alerts, and produces backtested three-year forecasts with honestly labelled uncertainty.

The strongest lesson was that correctness is a feature: grain rules, provenance, and reconciliation tests are what turn public disclosures into numbers a decision-maker can defend.

Engineering lessons: design safeguards structurally (flags and schema separation) rather than by convention; prefer simpler models when backtests say so; and report interval coverage honestly — 63% against an 80% nominal band is information, not failure.

Future work (not yet implemented): quarterly disclosure parsing when a defensible source exists, forecast reconciliation across platform scopes, scheduled monitoring notifications, and public deployment of the dashboard using the prepared read-only role.
